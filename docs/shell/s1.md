# S1: job control and pseudo-terminals

Phase S1 of the shell ([design.md](design.md)). What a shell needs of the
kernel before it can be written: Ctrl+Z, `fg` and `bg`, and a terminal that is
not the one console.

## Where it starts (2026-11-19)

- **SIGSTOP stops one task**, not the process: `t->signal_stopped` on the
  task that took it, SIGCONT clears it per task. No other stop signal exists:
  SIGTSTP, SIGTTIN and SIGTTOU are delivered with no default action.
- **wait does not hear a stop.** WUNTRACED, WSTOPPED and WCONTINUED report
  nothing, and the parent gets no SIGCHLD for a stop or a continue.
- **Ctrl+C is the keyboard driver's**, not the line discipline's: the arch
  raises SIGINT on the foreground group, with a fallback to the newest task
  when the group has none. Ctrl+\ and Ctrl+Z do nothing; a background read
  takes the input; TOSTOP means nothing.
- **There is one terminal**, the console, its line discipline written into
  `kernel/abi/files/console.c` with its state in one static. No `/dev/ptmx`,
  no `/dev/pts`, no controlling terminal per session.

## Steps

Each step is one commit, verified as every step here is: host tests, the
sabotage cases that prove them, six boots, and the step's LTP tests once.

1. **A stop is the process's.** SIGSTOP, SIGTSTP, SIGTTIN and SIGTTOU (the
   last three catchable, default action stop) stop every thread of the
   process; SIGCONT continues every one, and discards a pending stop as a stop
   discards a pending SIGCONT. The parent hears both: SIGCHLD with
   CLD_STOPPED or CLD_CONTINUED unless it set SA_NOCLDSTOP, and wait4/waitid
   report WUNTRACED/WSTOPPED (status `0x7f | sig << 8`) and WCONTINUED
   (`0xffff`), each once. SIGTSTP, SIGTTIN and SIGTTOU sent to a process in an
   orphaned process group are discarded, as Linux does, so that nothing stops
   with no shell left to continue it. Oracle: LTP's `waitpid*`, `waitid*`,
   `kill*` and `sigaction*` tests that stop and continue.

2. **The terminal's own signals.** The line discipline, not the keyboard
   driver, turns VINTR, VQUIT and VSUSP into SIGINT, SIGQUIT and SIGTSTP for
   the foreground group when ISIG is set, and flushes the line as Linux does
   (NOFLSH aside); the arch's newest-task fallback goes. A read from a
   background group is SIGTTIN (EIO when it is ignored or blocked, or the
   group is orphaned); a write with TOSTOP, and TCSETS or TIOCSPGRP from the
   background, are SIGTTOU. A session has a controlling terminal: setsid
   drops it, TIOCSCTTY takes it, TIOCNOTTY gives it up, TIOCGSID and
   TIOCGPGRP answer. Proved at boot by BusyBox's ash: a `sleep` stopped by a
   typed Ctrl+Z, `jobs` naming it, `fg` bringing it back.

3. **The line discipline out of the console.** `kernel/core/tty.c` (portable,
   host-tested): a terminal is an instance - modes, size, the line being
   typed, the foreground group, the session - fed characters by whatever is
   behind it. The console becomes one instance, behind the keyboard and the
   framebuffer. Nothing a program sees changes; it is the step that makes the
   next one possible, and the host tests reach a line discipline they could
   only reach through the console before.

4. **Pseudo-terminals.** `/dev/ptmx` and a `devpts` at `/dev/pts`: opening
   the master makes a pair, the master's writes are the slave's typing and
   the slave's output is the master's reading, through step 3's line
   discipline. TIOCGPTN, TIOCSPTLCK, TIOCGWINSZ/TIOCSWINSZ with SIGWINCH to
   the foreground group, and the master's last close a hangup: SIGHUP to the
   session, EIO to the slave's reads. Oracle: LTP's `pty*` and `ptem*`; at
   boot, BusyBox's `script` running a command on a pseudo-terminal.

## Status

Step 1: done 2026-11-19. SIGTSTP, SIGTTIN and SIGTTOU stop by default
(they killed before: Ctrl+Z would have ended the job), and a stop is the
process's: the first thread to take one records it on the leader
(`stop_sig`, `stop_report`), tells the parent - SIGCHLD with CLD_STOPPED
unless SA_NOCLDSTOP, and a wake out of wait - and sends SIGSTOP to every
other thread (`hw_group_stop`). SIGCONT, when it is sent and whatever its
disposition, continues every thread, throws away pending stops and tells the
parent CLD_CONTINUED; a stop sent throws away a pending SIGCONT. A
job-control stop in an orphaned group is discarded, SIGSTOP never. wait4
honours WUNTRACED and WCONTINUED (status `0x7f | sig << 8`, `0xffff`) and
waitid WSTOPPED and WCONTINUED with CLD_STOPPED/CLD_CONTINUED, each event
once, never reaped, looked at only with WNOWAIT - which closes the registry's
gap on waitid. At boot ash stops a background `sleep` with SIGTSTP and its
`jobs` says Stopped; the gate fails `job_not_stopped` and
`job_control_did_not_finish`. The self-test sleeps before the TSTP: a child
ignores SIGTSTP as ash does until it has put the default back, and a TSTP sent
before that is discarded, on Linux too - the first boot showed exactly that.
Host test `t_job_stop`; cases `sig-job-stop.txt`, `sig-job-stop-deliver.txt`
(host), `sig-job-stop-boot.txt` (boot); LTP `tests/corpus/ltp-s1.txt`: 35 of
39 pass, every stop and continue among them (`waitpid13`, the `waitid` and
`kill` tests); the four that do not are a core dump this kernel does not
write (`kill11`, `waitpid01`, `waitid10`'s `/proc/sys/kernel/core_pattern`)
and `rt_sigaction01`, whose wrapper reads a restorer musl does not fill
(docs/abi/phases.md, L2).

Step 2: done 2026-11-21. The line discipline decides what a control
character is. VINTR, VQUIT and VSUSP are SIGINT, SIGQUIT and SIGTSTP to the
foreground group under ISIG, once per process, what was typed thrown away
unless NOFLSH; with ISIG clear they are bytes, which is what a full-screen
program needs. They are judged at the keyboard's interrupt, so that a program
reading nothing can be reached, and again where input is taken from the queue
(the injected script, a serial line) - where taking stops at the signal, or a
killed `cat` takes the shell's next line with it. The newest-task fallback is
gone. A background read is SIGTTIN (EIO when ignored, blocked or orphaned); a
write under TOSTOP, TCSETS, TIOCSWINSZ and TIOCSPGRP are SIGTTOU. The terminal
belongs to a session: TIOCSCTTY, TIOCNOTTY, TIOCGSID, and ENOTTY for another
session's. The native shell had to learn what every job-control shell does -
ignore SIGTTOU, give the job the default back, `setpgid` from both sides -
or taking the terminal back from an ended job failed one boot in some, and its
next read was EIO. At boot a foreground `cat` is stopped by a typed Ctrl+Z,
returned by `fg`, ended by Ctrl+C, and a background `cat` stops on its read.
Host test `t_tty_jobs`; cases `tty-jobs.txt`, `tty-jobs-boot.txt`. Not yet: a
session leader's exit hanging up its terminal, and `/dev/tty` being ENXIO to a
process with no controlling terminal - both with step 4's hangup.

Steps 3 and 4: open.
